# Eastern Blue Tongue Skink
**Scientific name:** Tiliqua scincoides scincoides
**Difficulty:** Intermediate

## Overview
Eastern blue tongue skinks are heavy-bodied Australian lizards that usually reach 18-24 inches long. They have short strong legs, a triangular head, subtle banding, pale bellies, dark eye bands, and the bright blue tongue typical of the group. They occur in southeastern Australia in woodland, grassland, heath, and even suburban gardens. They are terrestrial, curious, and often personable, with lifespans of 15-20 years or longer.

## Enclosure
An adult should have an enclosure at least 48 inches long, 24 inches wide, and 24 inches tall, with larger housing preferred. Front-opening access and good ventilation are helpful. Blue tongue skinks are ground-focused and need floor space more than climbing height. Keep one skink per enclosure; Eastern blue tongues are territorial and may fight severely if housed together.

## Lighting and UVB
Eastern blue tongue skinks are diurnal and require bright daytime lighting plus UVB. Use a T5 HO desert UVB bulb such as a 10.0 or 12% lamp on the warm side. Aim for UVI around 3.0-4.0 at the basking area. Approximate basking distance is 7-11 inches through mesh or 12-18 inches when mounted inside. Use a regular daytime photoperiod and provide shaded areas for retreat.

## Heating
Provide a basking surface of 105-115°F and a cool zone of 70-80°F. Halogen flood bulbs over a broad basking stone or tile work well. Adjust wattage or dimming if temperatures are too high or low. Use an infrared thermometer to check surface temperatures and digital probe thermometers to track air temperatures.

## Humidity
Maintain average ambient humidity around 40-60%, measured near the cool side at ground level. Consistently higher or lower humidity can contribute to health and shedding problems. If humidity drops, lightly moisten part of the substrate or provide a slightly humid hide while keeping the overall enclosure well ventilated.

## Substrate
Use 4-6 inches of clean soil-like substrate. Eastern blue tongues naturally encounter sandy to clay-based soils, so organic additive-free topsoil or reptile soil products can work well. Avoid dusty or chemically treated materials. Sick or wounded skinks should be kept on paper towels until recovered. Spot-clean daily and replace substrate every 4-6 months as needed.

## Decor and Enrichment
Provide at least two hides, cork logs, cork flats, sturdy low branches, rocks, plants, and visual barriers. Blue tongue skinks enjoy pushing through cover and investigating their environment, so the habitat should not be bare. Keep climbing items low and stable because they are not agile climbers.

## Diet
Eastern blue tongue skinks are omnivores. Feed babies up to 3 months daily, juveniles 3-8 months about three times weekly, and subadults or adults over 8 months once or twice weekly. Skinks under 12 months should receive about 70-80% high-protein foods, while older animals do better with protein reduced to about 50-60%, with the rest mostly greens and vegetables. Protein options include high-quality dog or cat food without fish flavors, chicken hearts or gizzards, eggs, mice, roaches, grasshoppers, locusts, snails, hornworms, silkworms, and earthworms. Vegetables can include collards, cactus pads, spring mix, arugula, kale, pea shoots, bok choy, carrot greens, dandelion, and hibiscus. Fruit should be occasional.

## Supplements
Use calcium and multivitamin supplements, or a balanced all-in-one reptile supplement. Low-D3 or no-D3 calcium is typical when proper UVB is provided. Follow label directions and adjust for diet quality and veterinary guidance.

## Water
Provide a large, shallow bowl of fresh water every day. The bowl should be stable and easy for the skink to access. Clean it often because substrate is easily tracked into the water.

## Safety Notes
Secure heat sources and keep basking surfaces within range to prevent burns. Avoid cohabitation. Monitor weight closely, as adults can become overweight on frequent high-protein meals.
