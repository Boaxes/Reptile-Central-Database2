# Leopard Gecko
**Scientific name:** Eublepharis macularius
**Difficulty:** Easy

## Overview
Leopard geckos are small terrestrial geckos from arid and semi-arid regions of Afghanistan, Pakistan, India, and Nepal. Adults are usually 7-10" long, with eyelids, pebbled skin, short legs, and a fat segmented tail. Wild-type animals are yellowish with dark spots, while captive-bred morphs come in many colors and patterns. They often live 15-20 years with good care and are popular because they are hardy, manageable, and generally tolerant of gentle handling.

## Enclosure
Use at least a 36"L x 18"W x 18"H front-opening terrarium for one adult. Floor space is more important than height, but leopard geckos also use stable rocks and ledges. Cohabitation is not recommended for beginners, and males should never be housed together.

## Lighting and UVB
Provide a 12-hour day/night cycle. Leopard geckos are crepuscular, but low-level UVB supports healthy vitamin D metabolism and natural behavior. For normally pigmented geckos, target UVI 1.0-2.0 at the basking area. A 12" T5 HO 5.0 UVB over mesh or a 12" ShadeDweller-style lamp mounted inside can work, keeping the gecko at least about 10" from the bulb. Albino, patternless, and other low-pigment morphs need weaker exposure, around UVI 0.5-0.7.

## Heating
Provide a basking surface of 94-97 F, a warm hide around 90-92 F, and a cool zone of 70-77 F. Night drops to about 60 F are tolerated by healthy animals. A halogen heat bulb over a flat stone and warm hide is preferred. If the warm hide does not reach target temperatures, a thermostat-controlled heat mat may be used under the hide with substrate preventing direct contact.

## Humidity
Average ambient humidity should stay around 30-40%, with normal nighttime increases. Provide a humid hide on the cool side with moist substrate, targeting about 70-80% inside the hide. This supports shedding and hydration without making the whole enclosure damp.

## Substrate
Use a well-drained naturalistic arid substrate for healthy, well-hydrated geckos with proper heat. A mix of organic topsoil, play sand, and excavator clay is suitable, as are quality arid reptile substrates. Avoid loose substrate for sick, impacted, very young, or poorly heated animals. Spot-clean daily and fully replace non-bioactive substrate every 3-4 months.

## Decor and Enrichment
Provide at least a warm hide, cool hide, and humid hide, plus stable rocks, hollow logs, ledges, thick branches, and drought-tolerant live or artificial plants. Arrange cover so the gecko can move between heat and shade without crossing open space.

## Diet
Leopard geckos are insectivores. Offer appropriately sized live insects, about two insects per inch of gecko length or what the gecko eats in 15 minutes. Juveniles usually eat daily, young adults every 2-3 days, and adults with thick tails about every 5 days. Good feeders include crickets, dubia or discoid roach nymphs, red runner roaches, black soldier fly larvae, mealworms, darkling beetles, hornworms, silkworms, and grasshoppers. Variety is important.

## Supplements
Dust feeder insects with calcium to balance the calcium-phosphorus ratio. Use a multivitamin periodically, or a balanced all-in-one supplement, following label directions. Supplement choice should account for whether UVB is provided.

## Water
Keep a shallow dish of fresh water available at all times. Clean and refill it regularly.

## Safety Notes
Prevent falls from unstable decor and avoid excessive UVB for light-sensitive morphs. Do not pull on the tail; leopard geckos can drop it under stress. Persistent poor appetite, weight loss, stuck shed, or thin tail condition warrants veterinary attention.
