# Giant Leaf-Tailed Gecko
**Scientific name:** Uroplatus fimbriatus
**Difficulty:** Intermediate

## Overview
Giant leaf-tailed geckos are large arboreal geckos from northern and eastern Madagascar. Adults reach about 11-13" and have barklike or lichenlike camouflage, broad flat tails, pale eyes with red veining, no eyelids, and a fringe of skin that breaks up the body outline. They are sensitive display animals rather than hands-on pets. With stable care, they may live up to about 15 years.

## Enclosure
Provide a tall, spacious enclosure for one adult, at minimum 36"L x 18"W x 36"H or 24"L x 24"W x 48"H. Larger is better. Glass works well because these geckos climb smooth surfaces; PVC can also work, while screen cages are usually poor at holding humidity. House singly, as cohabitation can lead to fighting, injury, or death.

## Lighting and UVB
Use a 12-hour day length. Giant leaf-tailed geckos are crepuscular and nocturnal-leaning, but they still benefit from low, filtered UVB. A 22" T5 HO 5.0/6% UVB tube in a reflector can be used over the upper climbing area, overlapping the heat source. Keep the closest basking perch at least 6" below the lamp and aim for UVI 1.0-2.0.

## Heating
Keep heat gentle. Target a basking area of 80-82 F, average daytime air around 76-78 F, and a cooler lower zone of 70-74 F. In warm rooms, supplemental heat may be minimal; otherwise, a pair of low-wattage white incandescent bulbs can create a mild warm spot. Use vertical and diagonal branches at multiple heights so the gecko can thermoregulate.

## Humidity
Maintain about 60-70% humidity during the day and 75-85% at night. Mist morning and evening, or use an automatic misting system, while allowing some daytime drying. Measure humidity around the middle of the enclosure. A small water bowl should remain available even if the gecko mostly drinks droplets.

## Substrate
Use about 4" of moisture-retentive tropical substrate such as a topsoil/coconut fiber mix or a prepared tropical reptile bedding. Leaf litter helps retain moisture and supports a naturalistic setup. Spot-clean daily and replace contaminated areas promptly; full substrate replacement is usually needed every 3-4 months outside of bioactive systems.

## Decor and Enrichment
Design the habitat like a vertical forest edge. Use thick branches, cork, vines, and foliage from bottom to top, with many vertical resting surfaces. Include different branch widths and angles for exercise, but keep the layout calm and covered so the gecko feels secure during the day.

## Diet
Giant leaf-tailed geckos are insectivores and usually respond best to live prey. Juveniles can be fed daily; adults generally do well every other day, with occasional skipped meals acceptable when body condition is good. Offer as much as they will eat in a night without leaving excess insects loose. Use varied feeders such as crickets, dubia roaches, discoid roaches, red runner roaches, darkling beetles, captive-bred snails, and grasshoppers. Gut-load feeders for 24-48 hours before feeding.

## Supplements
Dust most feeder insects lightly with a quality calcium or all-in-one reptile supplement. Snails with intact shells generally do not need additional calcium dusting. Follow label directions and avoid heavy coating.

## Water
Provide drinking droplets through misting and keep a small dish of clean water available. Refresh water daily and ensure the enclosure has enough ventilation to prevent stagnant dampness.

## Safety Notes
This species is delicate, stress-prone, and poorly suited to routine handling. Avoid sudden temperature spikes, dry conditions, and rough capture. Handle only when necessary and support the body carefully.
