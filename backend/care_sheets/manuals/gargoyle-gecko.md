# Gargoyle Gecko
**Scientific name:** Rhacodactylus auriculatus
**Difficulty:** Easy

## Overview
Gargoyle geckos are medium-sized New Caledonian geckos, usually 6-8" long, with broad toe pads, a sturdy build, and a raised fringe running from the eyes down the back. Captive-bred animals occur in many colors and patterns. They come from humid forest habitat on New Caledonia, especially Grande Terre and the Isle of Pines. With good care they commonly live 15-20 years or longer. Their manageable size, calm nature, and prepared-diet options make them approachable for newer keepers.

## Enclosure
Use at least an 18"L x 18"W x 24"H front-opening terrarium for one adult. Taller, well-planted enclosures are better because this species is arboreal. Very small juveniles under about 12 g may do best temporarily in a smaller grow-out enclosure where they can find food easily. Cohabitation is not recommended for beginners; males must not be housed together, and even female groups require extra space and close monitoring.

## Lighting and UVB
Keep lights on a roughly 12-hour cycle. Gargoyle geckos are crepuscular, but low-level UVB is beneficial. In a standard adult enclosure, a 12" T8 5.0/6% UVB bulb in a reflector is appropriate when climbing branches keep the gecko at least 6" from the lamp. Target a basking UVI of about 1.0-2.0 near the upper perches.

## Heating
Provide a gentle warm zone rather than intense heat. Aim for an upper basking area of 82-85 F, a cooler lower zone of 70-75 F, and nighttime temperatures around 65-72 F. A low-wattage white incandescent bulb, often around 40 w, is usually enough. Use branches or foliage below the lamp so the gecko can choose warmer or cooler resting positions.

## Humidity
Maintain average humidity around 60-80%. Let the enclosure dry somewhat between mistings so it is humid but not constantly wet. Mist morning and evening with a pressure sprayer; tap water is acceptable for routine misting when local water quality is safe. Monitor with a digital hygrometer placed around mid-height.

## Substrate
A moisture-retentive tropical substrate helps stabilize humidity and cushions falls. Suitable options include soil/coconut fiber blends, reptile-safe tropical bedding, or bioactive-style mixes. Use 2-4" of substrate with leaf litter over the surface. Spot-clean daily and replace dirty substrate as needed; full changes are often needed every 1-3 months unless using a maintained bioactive system.

## Decor and Enrichment
Fill the enclosure densely with branches, vines, cork, bamboo, foliage, and arboreal hides. Provide cover at several heights and multiple daytime sleeping spots. A feeding ledge should be easy to reach without leaving cover. The goal is a layered climbing habitat, not an open glass box.

## Diet
Gargoyle geckos are omnivores that eat fruit-based foods and invertebrates. Offer a high-quality complete crested gecko diet: daily for hatchlings and juveniles, and every 2-3 days for adults. Provide live insects once or twice weekly for young geckos and about weekly for adults. Good feeders include crickets, dubia roaches, red runner roaches, beetles, snails, and grasshoppers. Occasional thawed pinky mice or small lizard prey may be used as rare treats for appropriate animals.

## Supplements
Complete gecko diets are already fortified. Dust feeder insects lightly with calcium, preferably without extra D3 when UVB is provided, following the product label. Avoid over-supplementing.

## Water
Misting provides much of their drinking water, but a small clean water cup should also be available. Replace food and water cups frequently to prevent spoilage.

## Safety Notes
Gargoyle geckos can drop or injure their tails and may bite if restrained. Handle gently, keep sessions short, and prevent falls. Watch cohabited animals closely for bullying, tail loss, bite wounds, or weight loss.
