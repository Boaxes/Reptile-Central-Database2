# Corn Snake
**Scientific name:** Pantherophis guttatus
**Difficulty:** Easy

## Overview
Corn snakes are active, semi-arboreal snakes native to the southeastern United States and nearby regions. They are usually 3-5 feet long, slender, and naturally patterned in orange, red, black, and white, though captive-bred morphs are widely available. They are popular because they stay manageable, feed readily, and usually tolerate calm handling. With good care, corn snakes may live up to 25 years.

## Enclosure
An adult corn snake should have an enclosure at least 48 inches long, 24 inches wide, and 24 inches tall. Larger is better, especially for active individuals. Use a secure, escape-resistant enclosure with front access if possible. Provide at least two hides, climbing branches, and enough cover for the snake to move without feeling exposed. Keep corn snakes singly; cohabitation creates competition and offers no welfare benefit.

## Lighting and UVB
Corn snakes are often active around dusk and night, but they benefit from a clear day/night cycle. UVB is recommended for overall welfare. Use a forest-strength UVB lamp long enough to cover about half the enclosure and place it on the warm side. Aim for UVI around 2.0-3.0 at the basking branch. Approximate distance is 7-10 inches through mesh or 11-16 inches when mounted inside. Use a timer for a consistent photoperiod.

## Heating
Create a gradient with a warm basking branch or surface around 90°F and a cool zone around 75°F. Night temperatures can safely fall to about 68°F. A pair of halogen flood bulbs can provide a broad basking area for an adult. Use dimmers or adjust wattage to fine-tune heat. Check basking surfaces with an infrared thermometer and use digital probes to monitor warm and cool areas.

## Humidity
Maintain average humidity around 65-75%, measured near the middle of the enclosure. It is normal for the cool side and nighttime readings to be higher than the warm side. Mist as needed, provide damp sphagnum moss in the cool hide, and use leaf litter to help hold moisture. Replace moss regularly so it does not mold.

## Substrate
Use 3-4 inches of a moisture-retentive, soil-like substrate. Reptile soil, jungle mix, coconut fiber, or a blend of organic topsoil, reptile soil, and play sand can work. Add clean leaf litter on top to provide cover and humidity support. Spot-clean daily and replace all substrate about every 3-4 months.

## Decor and Enrichment
Corn snakes climb readily and should have sturdy branches, cork logs, hides, plants, and visual barriers. Provide cover at several heights and temperatures. A cluttered, secure layout encourages exploration while still allowing the snake to retreat.

## Diet
Corn snakes are carnivores that eat whole prey. Feed hatchlings every 7-10 days, juveniles every 7-14 days, and adults every 14-21 days. A meal should total about 10% of the snake's body weight and should not be more than 1.5 times the snake's widest point. Mice are common, but variety is useful; options include appropriately sized mice, young rats, young quail, quail eggs, chicks, and small lizards from safe captive sources. Frozen-thawed prey is preferred and should be warmed to about 100°F before offering with tongs.

## Supplements
Whole prey usually provides complete nutrition. Occasional light dusting with a calcium and multivitamin blend can be used if the diet is limited, but supplements should be sparse and label-directed.

## Water
Provide a large bowl of clean water at all times. The bowl should be large enough for soaking and heavy enough not to tip easily. Replace water daily or whenever soiled.

## Safety Notes
Corn snakes are excellent escape artists, so secure every lid, door, and cable gap. Avoid live prey because it can injure the snake. Handle gently and avoid handling for about 24-48 hours after meals.
