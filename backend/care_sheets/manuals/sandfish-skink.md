# Sandfish Skink
**Scientific name:** Scincus spp.
**Difficulty:** Easy

## Overview
Sandfish skinks are small desert lizards famous for moving through loose sand as if swimming. They usually reach 6-8 inches long and have smooth scales, compact bodies, short legs, wedge-shaped snouts, fringed toes, and small dark eyes. The genus includes several species, such as the common sandfish, eastern sandfish, and Arabian sandfish.

They come from sandy deserts of northern Africa and the Arabian Peninsula. Sandfish are day-active but spend much of their time below the surface when not basking or hunting. They are hardy captives for keepers who enjoy natural behavior more than handling. With good care, they may live around 10 years.

## Enclosure
Use a front-opening, well-ventilated enclosure no smaller than 30 x 12 x 12 inches for one sandfish. Larger is better, especially if keeping more than one. Solitary housing is safest. A pair or small compatible group requires more room, such as at least 36 x 18 x 18 inches for two, and males should not be housed together.

## Lighting and UVB
Provide bright light for about 12 hours daily. Use a T5 HO 5.0 or 6% UVB lamp over the warm side. In a 12 inch tall enclosure with deep substrate, aim for a basking UVI around 2.0-3.0 and verify with a meter if possible. Add a separate 6500K LED or fluorescent daylight fixture to create strong visible light and encourage normal activity.

## Heating
Sandfish need intense surface heat above the sand. Use a halogen flood lamp to create a basking surface of 130-140°F with a cool zone around 80-90°F. In a shallow enclosure with 6 inches of substrate, a 90w PAR38-style halogen may be sufficient, but measure rather than guess. Use an infrared thermometer and adjust with a dimmer, fixture height, or wattage.

## Humidity
The main enclosure should remain dry, but sandfish still need access to moisture. Create a damp lower layer or a localized humid section by adding water through a vertical pipe to the bottom of the enclosure, or by partitioning off a small area of substrate and moistening it occasionally. The surface should not stay wet. Refresh the moisture weekly or every other week depending on drying speed.

## Substrate
Provide at least 6 inches of fine, loose sand so the skink can dive, tunnel, and rest below the surface. Reptile-safe fine desert sand is ideal; clean play sand can be used when needed. Remove visible waste daily and replace contaminated sand. Full substrate replacement about every three months helps maintain hygiene.

## Decor and Enrichment
Even though sandfish spend much of the day buried, decor still matters. Use cork flats, cork logs, sturdy branches, rocks, caves, and sparse live or artificial plants. Keep heavier items directly supported by the enclosure floor, not balanced on loose sand, so the skink cannot undermine them and become trapped.

## Diet
Sandfish are insectivores. Hatchlings under 3 inches may eat twice daily, juveniles around 3-5 inches daily, and adults at least 5 inches long every other day. Offer as many appropriately sized insects as the skink will eat in about five minutes. A few loose insects can be left briefly to encourage hunting, but remove uneaten feeders if they may bite or stress the skink.

Good feeders include crickets, dubia roaches, red runner roaches, discoid roaches, mealworms, darkling beetles, black soldier fly larvae, and small locusts. Rotate feeders to avoid nutritional gaps.

## Supplements
Dust insects with calcium and provide a reptile multivitamin or all-in-one supplement according to label directions. Gut-load feeder insects before offering.

## Water
Provide a shallow bowl of clean water at all times, even for this desert species. Expect to clean it often because sand will be kicked into the bowl.

## Safety Notes
Sandfish are not handling-oriented pets. Avoid compacted, damp, or dusty substrates that interfere with burrowing. Secure heavy decor at the bottom of the enclosure. Monitor heat carefully because the desired basking surface is very hot and overheating a small enclosure is easy.
