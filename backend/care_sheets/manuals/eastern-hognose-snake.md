# Eastern Hognose Snake
**Scientific name:** Heterodon platirhinos
**Difficulty:** Intermediate

## Overview
Eastern hognose snakes are burrowing snakes from the eastern United States and parts of southern Canada. They favor sandy, well-drained habitats such as open woodlands, meadows, prairies, and pastures. Adults may reach up to about 46" long, with females usually much larger than males. They are known for their upturned snout, keeled scales, dramatic defensive displays, and variable colors ranging from red and orange to brown, black, yellow, and greenish tones. Captive lifespan is commonly 10-15 years. They are compact and engaging, but feeding can be fussy, and their rear-fanged venom can cause painful localized reactions.

## Enclosure
Use at least a 36"L x 18"W x 18"H front-opening enclosure for one adult, with larger housing preferred for big females. Floor area matters more than height because this species spends much of its time underground. House eastern hognose snakes singly; cohabitation creates unnecessary competition for food, heat, hides, and security.

## Lighting and UVB
Provide a regular day/night cycle with about 12 hours of light daily. Because eastern hognose snakes are active during the day, bright visible light supports normal behavior and appetite. A T8 or T5 forest-strength UVB tube spanning about half the enclosure should be positioned on the warm side. Aim for a basking UVI around 2.0-3.0, adjusting distance for mesh obstruction. A separate 6000-7000K daylight or grow lamp can improve overall brightness.

## Heating
Create a warm basking surface of 86-88 F and a cool zone of 70-75 F. Use one or two white halogen or reptile heat bulbs over a stone basking surface so the snake can warm its body evenly. Measure surface heat with an infrared thermometer and monitor hide or ambient temperatures with digital probes. Turn heat lamps off at night unless the room drops below safe temperatures.

## Humidity
Maintain average enclosure humidity around 50-60%, with normal variation between the warm and cool sides and a rise at night. Mist lightly as needed and use a digital hygrometer probe near the middle of the habitat. Leaf litter helps buffer humidity while adding cover.

## Substrate
Provide at least 4" of diggable, tunnel-holding substrate. A soil-based reptile substrate or a mix of about 80% organic topsoil and 20% play sand works well. Add clean leaf litter on top. Spot-clean waste daily and replace contaminated substrate as needed; a full substrate change is typically needed every 3-4 months.

## Decor and Enrichment
Offer multiple snug hides, cork tubes, hollow logs, sturdy branches, plants, and surface clutter. Enrichment should give the snake choices between open basking, tight cover, and burrowing. Keep decor stable so tunnels and hides do not collapse.

## Diet
Eastern hognose snakes are carnivores and natural amphibian specialists. Feed appropriately sized whole prey about the width of the snake's head. Hatchlings may take pinky mice every 3-4 days, while adults may eat several small prey items every 4-5 days, adjusted for body condition. Frozen-thawed prey is safest. Mice, young rats, quail eggs, captive-bred frogs or toads, small lizards, or reptile sausage-style feeders can add variety. Frog or lizard scenting may help reluctant feeders.

## Supplements
Whole-prey diets usually do not require routine supplementation when varied and nutritionally complete. Not specified in the source notes beyond diet variety.

## Water
Keep fresh water available at all times in a bowl large enough for the snake to curl into if it chooses to soak. Clean and refill the bowl daily.

## Safety Notes
Eastern hognose snakes are rear-fanged venomous. Bites are not usually considered medically significant for humans, but swelling, pain, and discomfort can occur. Use feeding tongs, avoid smelling like prey during handling, and consider gloves for individuals that are food-motivated or defensive.
