# Boa Constrictor
**Scientific name:** Boa constrictor ssp. and Boa imperator
**Difficulty:** Intermediate - Hard

## Overview
Boa constrictors are large, muscular snakes from Central and South America. Many pet boas are Boa imperator or Boa constrictor constrictor, but several subspecies and localities exist. Most adults reach 5-8 feet, with females often larger than males and some individuals exceeding that range. Boas use both the forest floor and sturdy branches, especially when young. They are long-lived animals that may exceed 30 years with proper care.

## Enclosure
Young boas under 6 feet can be housed temporarily in a 4 foot by 2 foot by 2 foot enclosure, but adults need substantially more room. A practical adult minimum is 6 feet long, 3 feet wide, and 4 feet tall, with larger housing preferred. A useful minimum formula is snake length by half the snake's length by half the snake's length. Provide secure front access, ventilation, hides, and both floor and climbing space. Keep boas singly.

## Lighting and UVB
Boas are mostly crepuscular, but a defined day/night cycle supports normal rhythms. Provide about 12 hours of light per day. UVB is recommended for welfare even if boas can survive without it. Use a forest-strength UVB lamp spanning roughly half the enclosure on the warm side. Aim for UVI around 2.0-3.0 at the basking branch. Approximate distance is 7-10 inches through mesh or 11-16 inches when mounted inside, but measuring with a Solarmeter is best.

## Heating
Use halogen flood lamps to create a broad basking area, starting with two bulbs and expanding the cluster as the snake grows. Target a basking surface around 90°F, basking air around 80-85°F, a cool end of 75-80°F, and nighttime temperatures of 75-78°F. Place the basking area over a secure branch or platform that supports the snake's full weight. Check surfaces with an infrared thermometer and use digital probes for air and hide temperatures.

## Humidity
Maintain humidity around 55-75%, with the cool hide often being the most humid location. A humidity hide may run somewhat higher, up to about 85%. Mist with a pressure sprayer as needed, use moisture-retentive substrate, add leaf litter, and place damp sphagnum moss in the cool hide when extra humidity is needed. Replace moss before it molds.

## Substrate
Use 2-4 inches of soil-like, humidity-friendly substrate. Reptile soil, coconut fiber, natural bedding mixes, cypress-style mulch, or a mix of organic topsoil, reptile soil, and play sand can work. Add a generous layer of leaf litter. Spot-clean feces and urates promptly, remove contaminated substrate, and replace the full substrate every 3-4 months.

## Decor and Enrichment
Boas need secure hides, sturdy climbing structures, and visual cover. Use cork logs, large branches, caves, plants, and broad elevated resting areas. All branches and shelves must be firmly anchored because adult boas are heavy. Provide cover on both warm and cool sides so the snake can thermoregulate without feeling exposed.

## Diet
Boas are carnivores that eat whole prey. Feeding frequency slows with age: newborns and young juveniles often eat every 10-12 days, yearlings every 12-14 days, older juveniles every 2-3 weeks, and adults every 4-8 weeks depending on size, metabolism, and body condition. A meal should total about 10% of the snake's body weight and should not be wider than the snake at its widest point. Use varied prey such as mice, rats, African soft-furred rats, quail, and chicks. Frozen-thawed prey is preferred for safety and should be warmed before feeding.

## Supplements
Whole prey usually covers nutritional needs, but occasional light dusting with a calcium and multivitamin blend can be useful when the diet lacks variety. Use supplements sparingly and according to product directions.

## Water
Provide a large, sturdy bowl of fresh water at all times. The bowl should be large enough for soaking. Clean and refill it daily or whenever soiled.

## Safety Notes
Boa size varies by species, locality, sex, and individual growth. Plan adult housing before purchase. Use secure locks, anchored decor, and feeding tongs. Large boas should be handled with respect, and very large individuals are safest with another adult present.
