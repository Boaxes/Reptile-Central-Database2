# Dumeril's Boa
**Scientific name:** Acrantophis dumerili
**Difficulty:** Intermediate

## Overview
Dumeril's boas are terrestrial constrictors from southern Madagascar. They inhabit dry forests and spiny thickets and spend most of their time on or under cover at ground level. Adults commonly reach 4-8 feet, with females often larger than males. Their tan, reddish, and brown markings provide excellent camouflage in leaf litter. They are generally calm and slow-moving but need careful environmental management and a long-term commitment of 15-20 years or more.

## Enclosure
Match enclosure size to snake length. Babies under 2 feet can start in about 2 feet by 1.5 feet by 1.5 feet, juveniles and subadults from 2-5 feet need about 4 feet by 2 feet by 2 feet, adults around 6-8 feet need at least 6 feet by 2 feet by 2 feet, and very large adults need larger housing. The combined length and width of the enclosure should at least equal the snake's length. Floor space matters most, but 24 inches of height helps with substrate, low enrichment, and safe lamp distance. House singly.

## Lighting and UVB
Dumeril's boas are crepuscular but still benefit from a normal light cycle and low to moderate UVB. Use a T5 HO forest UVB lamp over the warm side and aim for UVI around 2.0-3.0 at the basking area. Approximate placement is 9-11 inches through mesh or 15-18 inches if mounted inside. Add visible daylight lighting if needed to maintain a clear daytime period.

## Heating
Target a basking surface of 86-88°F and a cool side/night range of 70-75°F. Daytime halogen flood lamps over the warm hide work well. Heat sources can usually turn off at night unless the room drops below about 70°F; in that case, use thermostat-controlled non-light-emitting heat such as a ceramic heat emitter or deep heat projector. If the warm hide is too cool, a thermostat-controlled heat mat under the hide can be used with substrate preventing direct contact.

## Humidity
Maintain average humidity around 40-60%, with temporary increases during shedding. Mist lightly in the evening as needed, add damp sphagnum moss to the cool hide, and use leaf litter to stabilize humidity. Avoid constantly wet conditions.

## Substrate
Use 2-4 inches of sandy soil or another moderate-humidity substrate. A mix of organic topsoil and play sand works well; some keepers use coconut husk, arid bioactive mixes, or clean natural sand blended with soil. Add a generous layer of leaf litter, as this species uses ground cover heavily. Spot-clean daily and replace all substrate about every 3-4 months.

## Decor and Enrichment
Provide snug hides, cork logs, leaf litter, low branches, plants, and ground-level cover. Dumeril's boas are not strong climbers compared with many boas, but low, sturdy climbing and textured obstacles still encourage movement. Keep decor stable and easy for a heavy snake to move around safely.

## Diet
Dumeril's boas eat whole prey. Feed hatchlings and juveniles up to about 3 feet every 7-10 days, subadults every 14-21 days, and adults every 21-28 days or as body condition requires. Meals should total around 10% of the snake's weight and should not be larger than 1.5 times the snake's widest point. Offer varied prey such as mice, domestic rats, African soft-furred rats, quail, and chicks. Frozen-thawed prey is safer than live and should be warmed before feeding with tongs.

## Supplements
Whole prey generally supplies complete nutrition. Occasional light dusting with calcium and multivitamin may be used if prey variety is limited, but supplementation should be infrequent and conservative.

## Water
Provide a large bowl of clean water at all times. Dumeril's boas may drink heavily, so check water daily. The bowl should be large enough for soaking and should be disinfected regularly.

## Safety Notes
Maintain secure locks and avoid live prey. Because this species spends so much time hidden, monitor body condition, sheds, and waste carefully rather than assuming a quiet snake is thriving. Handle gently and avoid handling shortly after feeding.
