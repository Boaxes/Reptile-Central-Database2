# Mossy Leaf-Tailed Gecko
**Scientific name:** Uroplatus sikorae
**Difficulty:** Intermediate

## Overview
Mossy leaf-tailed geckos are medium-sized arboreal geckos from forested regions of northern and eastern Madagascar. Adults are usually 6-8" long and have barklike or mosslike camouflage, a broad flat tail, a dark mouth lining, no eyelids, and a well-developed fringe of skin around the body. They are sensitive, low-handleability geckos best kept as display animals. Lifespan may reach about 15 years with stable husbandry.

## Enclosure
Use at least an 18"L x 18"W x 24"H front-opening enclosure for one adult, with larger or taller housing preferred. Glass helps maintain humidity and allows wall climbing; PVC can also work. Screen cages are usually too dry. House singly, as cohabitation can result in fighting and serious injury.

## Lighting and UVB
Use a 12-hour day length. Provide low-level UVB over the upper climbing zone, targeting UVI 1.0-2.0 at the basking area. A ShadeDweller-style T5 or suitable T8/T5 forest-strength lamp can be used depending on enclosure height, with the closest perch at least 6" from the bulb. UVB should overlap the gentle warm area.

## Heating
Provide a mild basking area of 82-84 F, average daytime air around 72-78 F, and a cool zone of 68-72 F. Low-wattage white incandescent bulbs are usually enough if the room is not already warm. Use thermometers to confirm conditions and provide climbing paths at multiple heights so the gecko can choose its preferred temperature.

## Humidity
Maintain high humidity: about 70-75% during the day and 80-85% at night. Mist morning and evening or use an automatic misting system. The enclosure should be humid but ventilated, with some drying between mistings. A small water dish should be available.

## Substrate
Use about 4" of moisture-retentive tropical substrate, such as a 60% organic topsoil and 40% coconut fiber mix or a commercial tropical reptile substrate. Add leaf litter to help maintain humidity and protect the surface. Spot-clean daily and replace dirty patches. Full replacement is usually needed every 3-4 months unless the enclosure is bioactive and well maintained.

## Decor and Enrichment
Provide a dense vertical layout with branches, vines, cork, and foliage. Mossy leaf-tailed geckos often rest pressed against vertical surfaces, so include upright branches and cork slabs as well as diagonal and horizontal routes. Foliage should create shaded retreats and visual barriers.

## Diet
Mossy leaf-tailed geckos are insectivores that usually need live prey to feed well. Juveniles can be fed daily; adults generally eat every other day. Offer a varied rotation of crickets, dubia roaches, discoid roaches, red runner roaches, darkling beetles, captive-bred snails, and grasshoppers. Gut-load feeders for 24-48 hours before offering them.

## Supplements
Dust most feeder insects lightly with calcium or a balanced reptile supplement according to label directions. Snails with shells can be offered without calcium dust. Avoid excessive supplementation.

## Water
Provide water through regular misting and keep a small bowl of fresh water available. Replace water daily and prevent stagnant wet areas.

## Safety Notes
Mossy leaf-tailed geckos are easily stressed by handling, heat, dehydration, and unstable conditions. Move slowly around the enclosure, avoid unnecessary capture, and watch closely for weight loss or reduced feeding.
