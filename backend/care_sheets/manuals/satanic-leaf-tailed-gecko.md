# Satanic Leaf-Tailed Gecko
**Scientific name:** Uroplatus phantasticus
**Difficulty:** Hard

## Overview
Satanic leaf-tailed geckos are small Madagascan geckos, usually around 4 inches long, with bodies and tails shaped and patterned like dead leaves. They have large eyes without eyelids, horn-like projections above the eyes, wide climbing toes, and delicate camouflage that makes them excellent display animals.

They live in forests along eastern Madagascar, using shrubs, saplings, and low tree growth. They are sensitive to poor conditions, do not tolerate heat well, and should not be treated as frequent handling pets. With careful husbandry, they may live up to about 15 years.

## Enclosure
A single adult should have at least an 18 x 18 x 18 inch front-opening terrarium. Larger is useful if it maintains proper humidity and gradients. Glass works well because these geckos climb smooth surfaces, though PVC can also be used with adequate ventilation. Groups may work if only one male is present, but add substantial space for each additional gecko and monitor closely.

## Lighting and UVB
This species is crepuscular to nocturnal, but low daytime UVB is still beneficial when provided safely. Use a 12-hour day cycle. A low to moderate forest-style UVB source can be used over the top of the enclosure, with climbing branches positioned so the gecko cannot get too close. Aim for a basking UVI around 1.0-2.0 at the upper usable perches. Measure when possible, because small enclosures can become overexposed quickly.

## Heating
Keep heat gentle. Target a basking perch of 80-85°F, average air temperatures of 68-77°F, and a cooler lower zone around 65-68°F. Many homes need little or no daytime heat for this species. If extra warmth is needed, use a low-wattage white incandescent or similar gentle heat source during the day only. Measure perches and air temperatures with reliable digital and infrared thermometers.

## Humidity
Maintain high humidity with a daily cycle: about 75-80% during the day and 90-100% at night. Mist heavily in the evening and again in the morning if needed, allowing some drying during the day. Good ventilation is still important; constantly stagnant wet air can cause problems. Tap water is acceptable for misting in many homes because it supplies minerals, though hard-water spotting may occur.

## Substrate
Use 2-4 inches of moisture-holding tropical substrate to stabilize humidity and cushion falls. A mix of organic topsoil and coconut fiber, reptile soil, or another tropical vivarium blend works well. Add leaf litter over the surface. Spot-clean waste daily and replace contaminated substrate; full replacement every 3-4 months is reasonable unless using a functioning bioactive system.

## Decor and Enrichment
Fill the enclosure densely with branches, vines, cork, and foliage at multiple heights. Include vertical, diagonal, and horizontal perches of different diameters. Live plants help maintain humidity and provide natural cover. The gecko should be able to hide during the day, climb without crossing large open spaces, and choose warmer, cooler, brighter, and darker areas.

## Diet
Satanic leaf-tailed geckos are insectivores and usually respond best to live prey. Juveniles can be fed daily, while adults can eat every other day, with occasional skipped meals acceptable for healthy adults. Offer what the gecko will consume overnight.

Good feeders include crickets, dubia roaches, discoid roaches, red runner roaches, darkling beetles, small captive-bred snails, and small locusts. Feeders should be appropriately sized and gut-loaded for 24-48 hours before feeding. Rotate prey types to keep the diet balanced.

## Supplements
Lightly dust feeder insects with calcium, except shelled snails. Use a reptile multivitamin or balanced all-in-one supplement according to label directions. Avoid heavy powdering that makes prey unappealing.

## Water
Mist foliage and enclosure surfaces so droplets are available for drinking, and keep a small clean water bowl available. Refresh the bowl daily.

## Safety Notes
Avoid high heat; temperatures that are comfortable for many other lizards can be dangerous for this species. Handle only when necessary and with care. Secure all climbing branches and avoid exposed bulbs inside the enclosure. Monitor hydration, body condition, and shedding closely.
