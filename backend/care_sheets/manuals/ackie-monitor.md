# Ackie Monitor
**Scientific name:** Varanus acanthurus
**Difficulty:** Intermediate

## Overview
Ackie monitors are small, active Australian monitor lizards that usually reach about 24 inches long. They have lean bodies, pointed snouts, forked tongues, strong limbs, and spiny tails. In nature they live in dry, rocky regions of northwestern Australia, using burrows and rock crevices for shelter and temperature control. They are intelligent, alert, and busy lizards that can be rewarding for keepers who want monitor behavior in a smaller package. With good care, they may live 15 years or longer.

## Enclosure
Provide a front-opening enclosure no smaller than 5 feet long, 2.5 feet wide, and 4 feet tall. A deep substrate dam is important because ackies need room to dig; plan for 12-24 inches of substrate depth. Larger enclosures are strongly preferred. Keep one ackie per enclosure unless the keeper is experienced, the enclosure is significantly larger, and animals can be separated immediately if conflict occurs.

## Lighting and UVB
Ackies are diurnal and need strong visible light plus high-quality UVB. Use a T5 HO desert UVB lamp on the warm side, such as a 12% desert bulb, and aim for a basking UVI around 4.0-6.0. Approximate basking distance is 7-9 inches below a lamp mounted over mesh or 12-15 inches below a lamp mounted inside the enclosure. A Solarmeter is the best way to confirm output. Run lights about 12 hours per day, or vary seasonally from roughly 11-13 hours. Add bright 6000-7000K daylight lighting across much of the enclosure to support normal activity and appetite.

## Heating
Create a strong heat gradient with halogen flood lamps over a basking stack. Target a basking surface of 158-172°F, warm-side air of 84-100°F, and a cool side of 75-82°F. Use an infrared thermometer for basking surfaces and probe thermometers for ongoing checks. A Retes-style stack made from secure layers of stone or wood gives the lizard multiple warm crevices and lets it choose the exact temperature it needs.

## Humidity
Ambient humidity can range from about 20-50%, but underground retreats should be much more humid, up to around 80%. Measure both surface-level air and burrow humidity with digital hygrometers. Add water directly into the substrate as needed so lower layers stay slightly humid without becoming wet or stagnant. Poor burrow humidity can contribute to dehydration and stuck shed.

## Substrate
Use fine sand or very sandy soil packed at least 12-18 inches deep; deeper is better, especially for females. Females should have at least 18 inches because of egg-laying needs. Suitable options include clean play sand, sandy soil mixes, or reptile desert substrates that hold burrows. Spot-clean feces and urates daily, remove contaminated areas, and replace all substrate every 6-12 months as needed.

## Decor and Enrichment
Build the habitat around climbing, digging, basking, and hiding. Use cork logs, cork flats, sturdy branches, rocks, ledges, plants, and secure background features. Heavy items should rest on the enclosure floor or be firmly supported so burrowing cannot collapse them. Provide multiple tight hiding spaces in both warm and cool areas.

## Diet
Ackie monitors are primarily insectivores. Feed hatchlings and juveniles daily, subadults and adults every other day, and reproductive females daily. Young and gravid animals can eat generously; established adults should receive what they can finish in about 5-10 minutes. Offer a wide variety of insects, including crickets, roaches, black soldier fly larvae, mealworms, superworms, beetles, hornworms, silkworms, grasshoppers, isopods, and similar prey. Occasional small vertebrate prey may be used sparingly.

## Supplements
Use a calcium supplement and a multivitamin, or a reputable all-in-one reptile supplement. Because UVB is provided, low-D3 calcium is usually appropriate unless a veterinarian or product schedule indicates otherwise. Dust feeders according to the supplement label and the animal's age, growth, and reproductive status.

## Water
Keep a shallow bowl of clean water available at all times. Replace water daily and clean the bowl regularly, even though the species comes from arid habitat.

## Safety Notes
Secure heat lamps and heavy decor so the monitor cannot burn itself or be trapped by shifting materials. Avoid cohabitation unless fully prepared to separate animals. Monitor body condition closely; ackies are eager feeders and can become overweight if adult portions are not controlled.
