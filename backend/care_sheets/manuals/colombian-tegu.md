# Colombian Tegu
**Scientific name:** Tupinambis spp.
**Difficulty:** Hard

## Overview
Colombian tegus are medium-large South American lizards, usually around 2-3.5 feet long. The most common species in captivity is Tupinambis teguixin, often called the gold tegu or Colombian black and white tegu. They come from northern South America, where they use humid forest habitats and may show semi-aquatic tendencies. Compared with Argentine tegus, they are often more defensive and many in the trade are wild-caught, so patience, quarantine, and experienced handling are important.

## Enclosure
An adult Colombian tegu should have a front-opening enclosure at least 6 feet long, 3 feet wide, and 3 feet tall, with larger housing preferred. Provide good ventilation while maintaining high humidity. Young animals can often live in adult-sized enclosures if there is enough cover. House Colombian tegus singly; they do not need companions, and forced cohabitation can result in stress or serious injury.

## Lighting and UVB
Colombian tegus are diurnal and should have bright visible light. UVB is strongly recommended. In a 6 foot by 3 foot by 3 foot enclosure, a long high-output desert UVB lamp over the warm side can be used, with the basking area roughly 24-30 inches below the lamp. Aim for UVI around 3.0-4.0 and verify with a Solarmeter when possible. Add 6000-7000K daylight lighting to brighten the habitat.

## Heating
Provide a large basking zone at one end of the enclosure. Target a basking surface of 125-135°F and a cool zone of 75-85°F. High-wattage halogen flood bulbs may be needed, and the basking area should be broad enough to heat the lizard's whole body. Use dimmers or lower-wattage bulbs if temperatures run too hot. Measure basking surfaces with an infrared thermometer and monitor air temperatures with digital probes.

## Humidity
Maintain high humidity, generally 70-80% at ground level on the cool side. Mix water into the substrate so it is evenly moist but not muddy, mist as needed, and provide a humid hide with damp sphagnum moss. The humid hide can be very humid, around 90-100%, but it must be cleaned and refreshed to prevent mold. For semi-aquatic individuals, provide a water basin large enough for the entire body.

## Substrate
Use at least 6 inches of clean, chemical-free soil-based substrate. Organic topsoil mixed with play sand works well, and reptile soil can be added for structure. Add leaf litter for cover and humidity. Spot-clean daily and replace the full substrate every 4-6 months as needed. Use paper towels instead of loose substrate for sick or wounded animals until recovery.

## Decor and Enrichment
Use large hides, hollow logs, tree stumps, sturdy branches, live or artificial plants, and visual barriers. Arrange the enclosure so the tegu can dig, patrol, hide, climb low structures, and soak. Heavy items must be stable and protected from being undermined by digging.

## Diet
Colombian tegus are primarily carnivorous, though older individuals may accept some plant matter. A practical target is about 90% animal-based foods and 10% vegetables or fruit. Feed hatchlings about five times weekly, juveniles four times weekly, subadults three times weekly, and adults twice weekly. Offer varied proteins such as crickets, roaches, black soldier fly larvae, mealworms, beetles, hornworms, silkworms, snails, grasshoppers, eggs with shell, chicks, rodents, shrimp, fish, and quality canned dog food. Vegetables can include collards, cactus pads, spring mix, arugula, kale, bok choy, carrot greens, dandelion, and hibiscus. Fruit should be occasional.

## Supplements
Use calcium and multivitamin supplements, or a complete reptile supplement. Choose low-D3 or no-D3 calcium when UVB is properly supplied, and follow label directions.

## Water
Provide fresh drinking water at all times. For many Colombian tegus, especially gold tegus, also provide a large soaking basin that can accommodate the whole body. Clean water containers frequently.

## Safety Notes
Expect defensive behavior, especially from wild-caught animals. Use secure locks, cautious handling, and a quarantine period with veterinary parasite screening. Do not underestimate bite strength or escape ability.
