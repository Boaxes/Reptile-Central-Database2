# Sudan Plated Lizard
**Scientific name:** Broadleysaurus major
**Difficulty:** Intermediate

## Overview
Sudan plated lizards are robust African lizards that may range from about 11-24 inches long. They have short legs, stout bodies, and rows of thick keeled scales. Color varies by locality, but many are dark brown with pale bellies and lengthwise yellow striping. They are also known as Broadley's rough-scaled plated lizards.

They inhabit rocky, semi-open areas of eastern and central Africa, including woodland, thicket, moist savannah, dry savannah, hillsides, termite mounds, and rock crevices. Captives may live more than 20 years. They are often shy and require patient acclimation, so they are better suited to keepers who enjoy observation and enclosure design more than frequent handling.

## Enclosure
Provide a front-opening enclosure no smaller than 48 x 24 x 24 inches for one adult. Larger is better because these lizards dig, climb, and run. Ventilation should be strong. Keep Sudan plated lizards singly; cohabitation can lead to competition, stress, and injury.

## Lighting and UVB
Use a 12-hour daytime cycle with bright visible light. Provide a 22 inch T5 HO desert-style UVB lamp, such as a 10.0 or 12% bulb, over the warm side. Aim for a basking UVI around 4.0-6.0. If mounted over mesh, the basking surface may need to be about 7-11 inches below the lamp; without mesh, about 12-18 inches is often appropriate. Confirm with a UV meter if possible. Add a 6500K LED or fluorescent daylight fixture to increase enclosure brightness.

## Heating
Use halogen flood lamps to create a broad basking zone. Target a basking surface of 105-115°F, a cool side of 75-80°F, and a nighttime drop around 60-72°F. A large flat basking stone under the lamps helps provide stable surface warmth. Measure with an infrared thermometer and use dimmers or wattage changes to adjust.

## Humidity
Provide a humidity range rather than one uniform value. Dry areas can sit around 20-40%, while humid retreats or moistened zones should reach about 50-80%. Evening misting once weekly and a cool humid hide with damp substrate can help. Use a digital hygrometer to confirm that both dry and humid choices are available.

## Substrate
Use at least 4 inches of sandy soil, deeper if possible, because plated lizards dig heavily. A suitable DIY mix is about 50% play sand, 30% organic additive-free topsoil, and 20% excavator-style clay, or use a comparable arid naturalistic substrate. Remove waste daily and replace the full substrate every 3-4 months unless a well-managed bioactive system is used.

## Decor and Enrichment
Offer cork logs, cork flats, sturdy branches, ledges, rock piles, hides, and live or artificial plants. Build stable crevices and cover so the lizard can retreat fully. Heavy decor should rest on the enclosure floor before substrate is added to prevent collapse during digging.

## Diet
Sudan plated lizards are omnivores. Hatchlings under 6 inches can receive insects and plant foods daily. Juveniles around 6-12 inches can eat insects five days weekly with plants daily. Young adults around 12-18 inches can receive insects about three days weekly and plants about four days weekly. Large adults often do well with insects twice weekly, plants four days weekly, and one fasting day.

Feed a varied mix of insects such as crickets, dubia roaches, red runner roaches, black soldier fly larvae, mealworms, darkling beetles, hornworms, silkworms, snails, and grasshoppers. Plant foods can include collard greens, cactus pads, spring mix, arugula, kale, pea shoots, alfalfa, bok choy, carrot greens, dandelion greens and flowers, hibiscus, and occasional fruit such as berries, figs, apple, papaya, mango, or prickly pear.

## Supplements
Use calcium and a reptile multivitamin, or a balanced all-in-one supplement, according to label directions. With good UVB, low-D3 or no-D3 calcium is usually appropriate unless a veterinarian advises otherwise.

## Water
Provide a shallow bowl of clean water daily. Routine soaking is unnecessary for a healthy plated lizard and can be stressful.

## Safety Notes
Secure heavy rocks and branches against digging. Do not cohabit. Give newly acquired or shy individuals plenty of cover and time before attempting handling. Monitor basking heat carefully so the animal can warm fully without being trapped in excessive temperatures.
