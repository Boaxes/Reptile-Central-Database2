# Northern Blue Tongue Skink
**Scientific name:** Tiliqua scincoides intermedia
**Difficulty:** Intermediate

## Overview
Northern blue tongue skinks are large, terrestrial Australian lizards that usually reach 18-24 inches long. They have heavy bodies, small strong legs, triangular heads, banded bodies, pale bellies, and yellow to orange markings along the sides. They occur in northern Australia across parts of Queensland, the Northern Territory, and Western Australia, where they use forests, woodlands, grasslands, gardens, and parks.

This species is popular because it is hardy, alert, and often becomes calm with regular gentle interaction. A well-kept northern blue tongue skink commonly lives 15-20 years and may live longer. They are poor climbers and swimmers, so husbandry should prioritize floor space, burrowing opportunities, and a secure thermal gradient.

## Enclosure
Provide a front-opening enclosure no smaller than 48 x 24 x 24 inches for one adult. Larger is strongly preferred for an active adult. Ventilation should be good, but the enclosure should also hold moderate humidity. House only one northern blue tongue skink per enclosure; this species is territorial and cohabitation can lead to serious injuries.

## Lighting and UVB
Use bright daytime lighting on a regular day cycle, about 12 hours on and 12 hours off. Provide UVB over the warm side with a 22 inch T5 HO desert-style lamp such as a 10.0 or 12% bulb in a reflector fixture. Aim for a basking UVI around 3.0-4.0. If the lamp sits over mesh, the basking surface may need to be about 7-11 inches below it; without mesh obstruction, about 12-18 inches is often appropriate. Confirm with a UV meter when possible.

## Heating
Use halogen flood lamps to create a warm basking zone. Target a basking surface temperature of 105-115°F and a cool side of 70-80°F. A broad piece of slate, flagstone, or similar stone works well as the basking surface. Measure surface temperature with an infrared thermometer and monitor the warm zone with a digital probe. Use dimmers or change bulb wattage to fine-tune the heat.

## Humidity
Maintain average ambient humidity around 40-60%, measured near the cool side at ground level. Short daily fluctuations are normal, but consistently dry or damp conditions can cause health problems. Adjust ventilation, substrate moisture, and light misting as needed.

## Substrate
Use 4-6 inches of packed sandy soil. A practical mix is about 60% organic additive-free topsoil and 40% play sand, or a similar arid naturalistic substrate. Spot-clean feces and urates promptly, remove contaminated substrate, and replace the full substrate every few months as needed. Use paper towels temporarily for sick or injured skinks.

## Decor and Enrichment
Provide at least two snug hides, one toward the warm side and one toward the cool side. Add cork, sturdy branches, rocks, slate, and durable live or artificial plants to break up sightlines and encourage exploration. Keep decor stable; these skinks are strong and may bulldoze loose items.

## Diet
Northern blue tongue skinks are omnivores. Babies up to 3 months can eat daily, juveniles around 3-8 months about three times weekly, and older juveniles to adults 1-2 times weekly. Young skinks should receive a higher-protein diet, roughly 70-80% animal-based foods during the first year. Adults generally do well with about 50-60% animal-based foods and the rest leafy greens and vegetables, with fruit only as an occasional treat.

Good protein options include quality dog or cat food without fish flavors, eggs, chicken hearts or gizzards, snails, dubia roaches, hornworms, silkworms, earthworms, grasshoppers, and occasional appropriately sized whole prey. Useful plant foods include collards, dandelion greens and flowers, arugula, spring mix, cactus pads, bok choy, kale, carrot greens, hibiscus leaves and flowers, and pea shoots. Offer variety instead of relying on one staple.

## Supplements
Use calcium and a reptile multivitamin, or a balanced all-in-one supplement, according to label directions. With proper UVB, choose low-D3 or no-D3 calcium unless a veterinarian recommends otherwise.

## Water
Keep a large, shallow bowl of clean water available at all times. Replace it daily or whenever soiled.

## Safety Notes
Avoid cohabitation. Prevent burns by keeping lamps outside the animal's reach and checking surface temperatures regularly. Do not keep recovering or wounded skinks on loose substrate. Because this is a long-lived reptile, plan for decades of space, food variety, and veterinary care.
