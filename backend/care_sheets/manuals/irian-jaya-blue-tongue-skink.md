# Irian Jaya Blue Tongue Skink
**Scientific name:** Tiliqua spp.
**Difficulty:** Intermediate

## Overview
Irian Jaya blue tongue skinks are Indonesian blue tongue skinks with unsettled taxonomy and variable appearance. They are typically 18-24" long, heavy-bodied, terrestrial lizards with short limbs, triangular heads, and blue tongues. Many show brown, gray, orange, or auburn body bands with speckled forelimbs, though pattern varies widely. They come from humid tropical regions and are often wild-caught in the pet trade. With good care, they commonly live 15-20 years or longer.

## Enclosure
Provide at least a 48"L x 24"W x 24"H front-opening enclosure for one adult, and use more floor space when possible. These skinks are active ground dwellers, not strong climbers or swimmers. Keep them singly; cohabitation can cause stress, bites, and serious injuries.

## Lighting and UVB
Use bright daytime lighting for about 12 hours per day. UVB is strongly recommended even if dietary D3 is used. A 22" T5 HO desert-strength UVB bulb on the warm side is appropriate for a 48" enclosure. Aim for UVI 3.0-4.0 at the basking area, with approximate distances of 9-14" through mesh or 15-24" when mounted inside without mesh obstruction.

## Heating
Create a basking surface of 100-105 F and a cool side of 70-80 F. Two 90 w PAR38 halogen flood bulbs over slate or flagstone often create an even basking zone for the skink's broad body. Use dimmers if temperatures run hot. Check surface temperature with an infrared thermometer and monitor ambient conditions with digital probes.

## Humidity
Maintain 60-80% average humidity, measured near the floor on the cool side. Mist in the morning and evening. If humidity remains too low, use a clean cool mist humidifier or fogger for a limited period after lights out. Use distilled water in foggers, disinfect them regularly, and avoid daytime fogging.

## Substrate
Use 4-6" of humid, soil-like substrate that supports burrowing and moisture retention. Good options include reptile soil, coconut fiber, tropical bedding, mulch, sugar cane mulch, or a mix of organic topsoil, reptile soil, and play sand. Add leaf litter and optional sphagnum moss. Use paper towels temporarily for sick, injured, or quarantined animals. Spot-clean daily and replace substrate every 4-6 months or as needed.

## Decor and Enrichment
Provide two or more large hides, cork flats, cork logs, sturdy plants, rocks, and visual barriers. Keep decor stable and low, since these skinks are strong but not graceful climbers. Arrange the enclosure so the animal can move between warm, cool, humid, and hidden areas without feeling exposed.

## Diet
Irian Jaya blue tongue skinks are omnivores. Feed babies daily, juveniles about three times weekly, and subadults/adults once or twice weekly. Young skinks need a higher-protein diet, roughly 70-80% protein foods during the first year. Adults should receive about 50-60% protein, with the balance from leafy greens and vegetables. Protein options include quality non-fish dog or cat food, eggs, chicken hearts, chicken gizzards, rodents, roaches, grasshoppers, locusts, snails, hornworms, silkworms, and earthworms. Plant foods can include collards, dandelion greens and flowers, cactus pads, spring mix, arugula, kale, pea shoots, bok choy, carrot greens, and hibiscus leaves or flowers. Offer fruit sparingly.

## Supplements
Use calcium plus a multivitamin, or a balanced all-in-one supplement, according to the product label. Adjust D3 use based on UVB exposure.

## Water
Keep a large shallow water bowl available at all times. Replace water daily and clean the bowl whenever soiled.

## Safety Notes
Because many Irian Jaya skinks are wild-caught, quarantine is important. Watch for parasites, dehydration, stuck shed, mites, and stress-related refusal to eat. Seek reptile veterinary care for new or declining animals.
