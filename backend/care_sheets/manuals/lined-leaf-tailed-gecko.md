# Lined Leaf-Tailed Gecko
**Scientific name:** Uroplatus lineatus
**Difficulty:** Intermediate

## Overview
Lined leaf-tailed geckos are arboreal geckos from northeastern Madagascar. Adults are about 10-11" long and have a striped or linear pattern in beige, brown, or mixed tones. They lack eyelids, have broad toe pads, a flat tail, and eyelash-like crests over the eyes. They are best treated as sensitive display animals, not routine handling pets. With good care they may live up to about 15 years.

## Enclosure
Use at least an 18"L x 18"W x 36"H enclosure for one adult, with taller or wider housing preferred. Glass is useful because these geckos climb smooth surfaces and need retained humidity; PVC can work, while screen enclosures are usually too drying. Keep only one lined leaf-tailed gecko per enclosure to avoid fighting and injuries.

## Lighting and UVB
Provide a 12-hour light cycle. Low-level UVB is recommended. A 12" T5 HO 5.0/6% UVB tube in a reflector over the upper climbing area can work, with the closest perch at least 6" below the lamp. Target UVI 1.0-2.0 near the basking area. Position UVB so it overlaps the gentle heat source.

## Heating
Aim for a basking area of 80-82 F, average air temperatures of 75-78 F, and a cool zone of 72-75 F. If the room is already warm, little supplemental heat may be needed. Otherwise, use low-wattage white incandescent bulbs to make a mild warm perch. Provide branches and foliage at multiple levels for thermoregulation.

## Humidity
Maintain roughly 70% humidity during the day and around 80% at night. Mist morning and evening, or use an automatic misting system. Allow some airflow and daytime drying so the habitat does not become stagnant. A small water dish should remain available even when the gecko primarily drinks droplets.

## Substrate
Use about 4" of moisture-retentive tropical substrate, such as a soil/coconut fiber mix or a prepared tropical reptile bedding. Add a thick layer of leaf litter to preserve humidity and create a natural floor. Spot-clean daily and replace contaminated substrate. Full substrate changes are typically needed every 3-4 months outside of bioactive systems.

## Decor and Enrichment
Build a vertical forest habitat with branches, vines, bamboo rods, cork, and live or artificial foliage. Lined leaf-tailed geckos often rest vertically, so include many upright surfaces, plus diagonal and horizontal routes of varied thickness. Dense plant cover helps them feel secure during daylight.

## Diet
Lined leaf-tailed geckos are insectivores and should be fed live prey. Juveniles may eat daily; adults usually eat every other day, with occasional skipped meals acceptable when body condition is good. Offer what they will consume overnight. Suitable feeders include crickets, dubia roaches, discoid roaches, red runner roaches, darkling beetles, captive-bred snails, and grasshoppers. Gut-load insects for 24-48 hours before feeding.

## Supplements
Dust most feeders lightly with calcium or a balanced low-D3 supplement, following label directions. Snails with intact shells generally do not need dusting. Avoid heavy supplement coatings that discourage feeding.

## Water
Mist to provide drinking droplets, and keep a small clean water bowl available. Refresh water daily.

## Safety Notes
Avoid handling except when necessary. This species is stress-sensitive and can decline with overheating, poor humidity control, or repeated disturbance. Quarantine new animals and monitor feeding closely.
