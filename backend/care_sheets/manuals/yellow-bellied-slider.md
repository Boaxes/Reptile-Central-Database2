# Yellow-Bellied Slider
**Scientific name:** Trachemys scripta scripta
**Difficulty:** Hard

## Overview
Yellow-bellied sliders are semi-aquatic turtles from the southern United States. Adults usually reach 8-11 inches, with females often larger than males. They are typically dark green and yellow with marbled markings, two dark spots on the front plastron scutes, and a broad vertical yellow bar behind each eye.

They inhabit ponds, lakes, swamps, and slow rivers, spending most of their time in water but basking on logs and rocks. They can live 20-30 years or longer. Their care is expensive and labor-intensive because adults need large water volume, powerful filtration, proper lighting, and a substantial dry basking area.

## Enclosure
Plan for at least 10 gallons of water per inch of shell length. Most adults need roughly 80-110 gallons of water or more. Aquariums, stock tanks, indoor ponds, or outdoor ponds can work if secure and escape-proof. Large tubs and ponds are often more practical than narrow aquariums. Keep one slider per enclosure; indoor cohabitation frequently causes bullying, stress, and competition.

## Lighting and UVB
Use a 12-hour daytime cycle. Provide a T5 HO 5.0 or 6% UVB lamp in a reflective fixture over the basking platform. In a 48 inch setup, a 34 inch lamp about 13-14 inches above the basking surface is a common starting point for a basking UVI around 3.0-4.0, but measuring is best.

Add a bright 6500K LED or fluorescent daylight lamp across about half to three quarters of the enclosure. This improves visible brightness and supports live plants, but it does not replace UVB.

## Heating
Provide a dry basking area and heated water. Target a basking surface of 95-104°F, basking air of 85-90°F, and water temperature of 74-76°F. Use splash-resistant halogen basking lamps to warm a platform large enough for the whole turtle to leave the water and dry. Measure basking surfaces with an infrared thermometer and water with a submersible digital thermometer. Use a heater rated for the full water volume and protect it with a guard.

## Humidity
Aquatic turtle enclosures naturally have high moisture nearby, but the basking zone must dry well. Keep the air above the platform ventilated and warm enough for the shell and skin to dry completely.

## Substrate
Bare-bottom water areas are easiest to maintain. If substrate is used, choose sand or rocks too large to swallow. Avoid gravel and small pebbles because ingestion can cause serious illness. A land section can be filled with play sand and fitted with a flat stone under the basking lamps.

## Decor and Enrichment
Use large stable rocks, driftwood, underwater hides, live plants, floating items, and safe climbing surfaces. Decor should not trap the turtle underwater or create sharp edges. Compatible fish may be used as enrichment, but assume they may be eaten.

## Diet
Yellow-bellied sliders are omnivores. Turtles under 1 year can receive about 50% protein and 50% vegetables, with both offered daily and pellets every other day. Adults over 1 year should shift toward about 25% protein and 75% vegetables, with protein 1-2 times weekly, vegetables daily, and pellets 2-3 times weekly.

Protein portions should be what the turtle can finish in 5-10 minutes. Vegetable portions can be about the size of the shell, and pellet portions about the size of the head. Protein options include crickets, earthworms, dubia roaches, silkworms, snails, frozen bloodworms, freeze-dried shrimp or krill, guppies, mollies, platies, and mosquito fish. Plant foods include collards, dandelion greens and flowers, endive, green or red leaf lettuce, romaine, kale, grated squash, carrot, green beans, and grated sweet potato. Fruit such as apple, berries, melon, or papaya should be occasional.

## Supplements
Good turtle pellets provide much of the vitamin and mineral base, but calcium should be available as cuttlebone. Remove the hard backing before offering it. Replace cuttlebone when consumed or fouled, often every 1-2 months.

## Water
Water quality is central to slider care. Use a canister filter rated for at least 2-3 times the actual water volume. Replace evaporated water, and perform about 30% water changes every 1-2 weeks depending on water tests and waste load. A siphon or pump makes maintenance easier. Disinfect sinks, tubs, or tools that contact dirty turtle water.

## Safety Notes
Do not release pet sliders outdoors. Use GFCI protection for electrical equipment near water. Prevent contact burns from heaters and lamps, secure all basking fixtures against splashing, and wash hands after handling turtles or enclosure equipment.
