# Jackson's Chameleon
**Scientific name:** Trioceros jacksonii ssp.
**Difficulty:** Hard

## Overview
Jackson's chameleons are cool-climate, arboreal lizards from Kenya and Tanzania, with established invasive populations in Hawaii. Adults are usually 7-14" long. Most are green with darker patterning, and males often have three forward-pointing horns while females may have reduced horns or none. They have independently moving eyes, gripping zygodactyl feet, and a prehensile tail. Males may live up to about 9 years, while females more often live 5-6 years. They are fascinating display animals but require precise temperature, hydration, and ventilation.

## Enclosure
Use at least a 24"L x 24"W x 48"H enclosure for one adult, with larger preferred. Height, ventilation, drainage, and dense cover are all important. Mesh enclosures work well in many homes, but covering the back and one side with thin plastic panels can reduce stress and help retain humidity. Young chameleons under about 10 months may start in a smaller 16"L x 16"W x 30"H setup. House singly.

## Lighting and UVB
Provide 12 hours of light daily. Use a 22" T5 HO 5.0/6% UVB bulb in a reflector across the top, with the basking branch arranged so the chameleon's back is about 6" below the top mesh. Target UVI 2.0-3.0 at basking height. Add a bright 6500K daylight or plant-growth lamp across much of the enclosure to support activity and plant growth.

## Heating
Jackson's chameleons need mild basking and cool nights. Aim for a basking area of 80-85 F, general daytime temperatures of 68-75 F, and nighttime temperatures of 50-65 F. A low-wattage white heat bulb, often around 40 w, is usually enough. Place a stable branch below the heat source and provide many cooler perches below it. Measure basking temperature at perch level and monitor gradients throughout the cage.

## Humidity
Target about 40% daytime humidity and 75-100% at night. Mist early morning and evening, ideally around lights-out or before lights-on, and add a light afternoon misting or dripper if needed. A cool mist humidifier or fogger may be used before sunrise, but only with clean equipment and distilled water. Avoid daytime fogging, which can increase respiratory risk.

## Substrate
Traditional loose substrate is usually impractical because chameleon setups receive heavy water. Use a drainage layer, bare drain tray, or custom drain system that moves water into a bucket or reservoir. Keep the floor clean and dry enough to prevent standing water and bacterial buildup.

## Decor and Enrichment
Use a dense network of branches, vines, and foliage from bottom to top. Live potted or hanging plants are strongly preferred because they hold drinking droplets and create visual cover. Leave one clear basking path under the heat and UVB, but provide many shaded hiding and travel routes.

## Diet
Jackson's chameleons are insectivores. Juveniles may eat as much as they can safely consume, while adults over 1 year usually do well on 3-5 appropriately sized insects every other day. Offer variety: crickets, dubia roaches, red runner roaches, black soldier flies and larvae, blue bottle flies and larvae, hornworms, silkworms, locusts, mantis hatchlings, stick insects, and captive-bred snails. Gut-load feeders for 24-48 hours before feeding.

## Supplements
Use supplements lightly. Dust feeders with plain calcium at most feedings, and use a multivitamin containing D3 and vitamin A about once monthly unless a veterinarian recommends otherwise. Jackson's chameleons can be sensitive to oversupplementation.

## Water
Most drinking comes from droplets on leaves after misting. A dripper in the mid to late afternoon can add a reliable drinking opportunity. Do not rely on a standing bowl as the primary water source.

## Safety Notes
Avoid overheating, stagnant wet air, and frequent handling. Stress can quickly affect appetite and health. Ensure drainage cannot overflow, keep foggers sanitary, and monitor females closely because reproductive stress can shorten lifespan.
