# Indonesian Blue Tongue Skink
**Scientific name:** Tiliqua gigas ssp.
**Difficulty:** Intermediate

## Overview
Indonesian blue tongue skinks are heavy-bodied, terrestrial lizards from humid Indonesian habitats. Adults are commonly 18-24" long, with short strong legs, triangular heads, and a distinctive blue tongue. Common pet forms include classic Indonesian, Halmahera, Kei Island, and Merauke types. They are generally curious and hardy once established, but correct humidity is essential. Lifespan is often 15-20 years or more with good care.

## Enclosure
Use at least a 48"L x 24"W x 24"H front-opening enclosure for one adult, with larger strongly preferred. Good ventilation matters, but the setup must also hold tropical humidity. Keep only one skink per enclosure, as blue tongue skinks can fight severely when housed together.

## Lighting and UVB
Indonesian blue tongue skinks are diurnal and benefit from bright daytime lighting. Provide a 12-hour light cycle. Use a 22" T5 HO desert-strength UVB tube on the warm side. Adjust basking distance for mesh: roughly 9-14" below UVB when mounted over mesh or 15-24" when mounted inside, targeting UVI 3.0-4.0 at the basking area.

## Heating
Create a strong basking surface of 100-105 F with a cool side of 70-80 F. Two PAR38 halogen flood bulbs over a large slab of slate or flagstone often work well in a 24" tall enclosure. Use dimmers or thermostatic control as needed, and verify the basking surface with an infrared thermometer. Track ambient temperatures with digital probes.

## Humidity
Most Indonesian blue tongue skinks need 60-80% average ambient humidity measured near the cool-side floor. Halmahera animals require much higher humidity, often 80-100%. Mist morning and evening. If needed, run a cool mist humidifier or fogger for a few hours after lights-out to mimic nighttime humidity rises. Use distilled water in foggers and disinfect equipment regularly; do not fog during the day.

## Substrate
Provide 4-6" of moisture-holding, soil-like substrate with leaf litter. Commercial tropical soil blends, coconut fiber, cypress-style mulch, sugar cane mulch, or a mix of organic topsoil, reptile soil, and play sand can work. Add sphagnum moss if extra humidity support is needed. Sick or wounded animals should be kept on paper towels until recovered. Spot-clean daily and replace substrate every 4-6 months or as needed.

## Decor and Enrichment
Offer at least two large hides, cork logs or flats, stable rocks, sturdy branches, and hardy live or artificial plants. Enrichment should create cover, scent opportunities, and barriers that let the skink move between warm, humid, and secure zones.

## Diet
Blue tongue skinks are omnivores. Babies up to 3 months can eat daily, juveniles around 3-8 months about three times weekly, and older subadults/adults once or twice weekly. Animals under 12 months should receive about 70-80% protein foods; adults should receive about 50-60% protein, with the rest mostly leafy greens and vegetables. Protein options include quality non-fish dog or cat food, eggs, chicken hearts or gizzards, rodents, dubia roaches, grasshoppers, locusts, snails, hornworms, silkworms, and earthworms. Greens and vegetables can include collards, arugula, spring mix, cactus pads, dandelion, bok choy, carrot greens, and similar produce. Fruit is a treat only.

## Supplements
Use calcium and a multivitamin, or a balanced all-in-one reptile supplement, according to label directions. Supplement frequency should account for UVB exposure and diet variety.

## Water
Provide a large, shallow bowl of fresh water at all times. Clean it daily, as skinks may walk through or soil the dish.

## Safety Notes
Imported or recently acquired animals may carry parasites or arrive dehydrated. Quarantine new skinks, arrange veterinary testing when possible, and monitor humidity carefully to prevent shedding and respiratory problems.
