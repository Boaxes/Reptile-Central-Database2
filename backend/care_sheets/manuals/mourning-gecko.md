# Mourning Gecko
**Scientific name:** Lepidodactylus lugubris
**Difficulty:** Easy

## Overview
Mourning geckos are tiny, fast, social geckos found across many tropical regions, including South Pacific islands, parts of Central and South America, and Hawaii. Adults are about 4" long, usually tan to brown with a pale belly and dark chevron or zig-zag markings. They climb smooth surfaces, lack eyelids, and are best kept as display animals rather than handled pets. They often live around 10 years. Most are female and reproduce by parthenogenesis, so groups can grow quickly.

## Enclosure
Keep mourning geckos in groups of at least two. A 12"L x 12"W x 18"H front-opening terrarium can house up to about four geckos, but larger is better and future offspring may require upgrades. Add roughly 5 gallons of capacity for every additional two geckos. The enclosure must be escape-resistant; even tiny gaps can allow juveniles to leave.

## Lighting and UVB
Use a regular day/night cycle. Mourning geckos are crepuscular, with activity around dusk and dawn. UVB is beneficial, especially because females produce eggs frequently. In a small vertical enclosure, a compact 5.0 forest UVB bulb in a reflective hood can work if basking areas are no closer than about 2" from the lamp. Target UVI 1.0-2.0 near the top.

## Heating
Provide a warm upper basking area of 80-85 F, a cooler zone of 70-75 F, and nighttime temperatures of 65-72 F. A 25 w white incandescent bulb is often enough. Avoid sustained temperatures above 85 F, which can be dangerous, and avoid keeping them chronically at or below 72 F. Use vines, twigs, and foliage at several heights so the geckos can move through the gradient.

## Humidity
Maintain 60-80% average humidity. Mist morning and evening with tap or spring water when safe for local use. Humidity should rise after misting and fall somewhat between sessions. Monitor with a digital hygrometer around the middle of the terrarium.

## Substrate
Use 2-4" of moisture-retentive tropical substrate, such as a topsoil/coconut fiber blend or commercial tropical reptile bedding. Add leaf litter on top. Substrate helps stabilize humidity and cushions falls. Spot-clean waste daily and replace contaminated material; full substrate changes are usually needed every 1-3 months unless bioactive.

## Decor and Enrichment
Pack the enclosure with fine branches, twigs, vines, cork hollows, arboreal hides, and live or artificial foliage. Provide many narrow climbing routes and hiding spots near feeding ledges. Dense cover is especially important because the geckos are small, quick, and easily startled.

## Diet
Mourning geckos are omnivores. Offer a complete crested gecko diet and replace it every 24-48 hours. It is fine to skip a prepared-diet feeding occasionally. Provide small calcium-dusted insects once or twice weekly, such as tiny crickets, small roaches, darkling beetles, snails, or appropriately sized grasshoppers. Feeders must be small enough for the geckos to subdue safely.

## Supplements
Prepared gecko diet is fortified. Dust live insects lightly with calcium, preferably without extra D3 when UVB is provided, following label directions.

## Water
Most drinking comes from mist droplets, but a very small water cup can be offered. Refresh water and food cups often to prevent mold and bacterial buildup.

## Safety Notes
Do not handle mourning geckos casually; they are tiny, fast, and fragile. Plan for reproduction by removing or managing eggs if needed. Check latches, vents, and cable openings carefully to prevent escapes.
