# Bearded Dragon
**Scientific name:** Pogona vitticeps
**Difficulty:** Intermediate

## Overview
Bearded dragons are robust Australian lizards that usually reach 18-24 inches long. They have spiny scales, a broad triangular head, and an expandable throat area that can darken when the animal is displaying or stressed. They come from dry open habitats, scrub, and woodland edges in central and eastern Australia. They are active by day, use both ground and climbing space, and often become calm, responsive pets. With strong husbandry, they commonly live 10-15 years and may live longer.

## Enclosure
An adult bearded dragon should have an enclosure at least 6 feet long, 2 feet wide, and 2 feet tall, with larger housing preferred. Front-opening access and good ventilation are important because the habitat must be bright and warm without overheating. Keep only one bearded dragon per enclosure. They are territorial, and cohabitation can lead to chronic stress, bullying, bites, and severe injury.

## Lighting and UVB
Bearded dragons require intense visible light and high-quality UVB. Use a T5 HO desert UVB bulb, such as a 12% or 10.0 lamp, placed over the warm side. Aim for basking UVI around 4.0-4.5. Approximate basking distance is about 11 inches through mesh or 16-17 inches for an internal unobstructed lamp. Add bright 6500K daylight or plant lighting across most of the enclosure length to encourage normal activity, feeding, and basking behavior. Provide a regular daytime photoperiod.

## Heating
Use a cluster of halogen flood bulbs over a large basking surface so the dragon's whole body can warm evenly. Target a basking surface temperature of 105-115°F and a cool zone of 70-85°F. If the cool side is too cold, add thermostat-controlled ambient heat such as a ceramic heat emitter, deep heat projector, or radiant heat panel. Turn visible and basking heat off at night unless room temperatures fall below a safe range. Measure basking surfaces with an infrared thermometer and monitor air temperatures with digital probes.

## Humidity
Keep average ambient humidity around 30-60%, measured near the cool side at ground level. Brief spikes or dips are not usually a problem, but consistently damp or extremely dry conditions can cause health issues. Light misting once or twice a week may help hydration and natural behavior without making the enclosure wet.

## Substrate
Healthy adult bearded dragons can be kept on packed fine sand or sandy soil at least 4 inches deep, with deeper substrate preferred for digging. A suitable DIY mix can include play sand, organic topsoil, and excavator clay. Clean commercial desert substrates may also work. Do not keep sick, weak, or wounded dragons on loose substrate; use paper towels until recovered. Spot-clean daily and replace the substrate every 4-6 months depending on enclosure hygiene.

## Decor and Enrichment
Provide a full, functional layout rather than an open box. Use cork logs, cork flats, sturdy branches, rocks, hides, textured backgrounds, and live or artificial plants. Include secure climbing areas, a broad basking platform, shaded retreats, and visual barriers. Heavy rocks should be placed so digging cannot make them shift or collapse.

## Diet
Bearded dragons are omnivores, with diet shifting as they mature. Hatchlings should receive insects twice daily plus vegetables daily. Juveniles usually eat insects once daily plus vegetables daily. Adults should receive insects 2-3 times weekly and vegetables daily. Protein should make up about 60-80% of the diet for hatchlings, 50-60% for juveniles, and 20-30% for adults. Offer varied insects such as crickets, roaches, black soldier fly larvae, mealworms, beetles, hornworms, silkworms, snails, and grasshoppers. Good plant foods include collards, cactus pads, spring mix, arugula, kale, pea shoots, bok choy, carrot greens, dandelion, and hibiscus. Fruit should be rare or avoided.

## Supplements
Use calcium and multivitamin supplements, or a complete reptile supplement system. With proper UVB, plain calcium or low-D3 calcium is usually appropriate for routine use, while multivitamins should follow label directions. Avoid excessive supplementation.

## Water
Provide a shallow dish of fresh water daily. Some dragons drink rarely from bowls, but water should still be available. Routine forced baths are unnecessary and should not replace correct humidity, diet, and drinking access.

## Safety Notes
Guard heat sources and check basking temperatures often; overheated basking sites can burn feet and bellies. Watch body condition because adults can become obese if fed like juveniles. Avoid loose substrate for animals that are ill, dehydrated, or not eating normally.
