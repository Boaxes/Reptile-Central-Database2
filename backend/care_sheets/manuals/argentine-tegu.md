# Argentine Tegu
**Scientific name:** Salvator spp.
**Difficulty:** Hard

## Overview
Argentine tegus are large, powerful South American lizards that often reach 3-5 feet long. Commonly kept species include the Argentine black and white tegu, Salvator merianae, and the red tegu, Salvator rufescens; hybrids also occur in captivity. They are terrestrial, highly active, intelligent, and long-lived, commonly reaching 15-20 years with good care. Well-socialized individuals can become calm and interactive, but their size, strength, appetite, and enclosure needs make them demanding pets.

## Enclosure
An adult Argentine tegu needs a front-opening enclosure at least 8 feet long, 4 feet wide, and 4 feet tall, with larger housing preferred. Good ventilation is necessary, but the enclosure must still hold warmth and humidity. Young tegus can often use adult-sized housing if the layout includes enough cover. House tegus singly; they are not social animals, and cohabitation can lead to stress, competition, and injury.

## Lighting and UVB
Argentine tegus are diurnal and benefit from bright daytime lighting. Provide strong UVB over the warm side, ideally using two long T5 HO desert bulbs in a large enclosure. Aim for a basking UVI around 3.0-4.0, confirmed with a Solarmeter when possible. UVB is strongly recommended even though their varied diet may help them survive without it. Add bright 6000-7000K full-spectrum visible lighting to improve daytime activity and create a more natural photoperiod.

## Heating
Use a large cluster of halogen flood lamps to warm the whole basking area, not just one small spot. Target a basking surface temperature of 125-135°F and a cool zone of 75-85°F. Because adult tegus have broad bodies, a cluster of at least four lamps is often needed in a large enclosure. Place the basking area at one end to create a clear gradient. Check surfaces with an infrared thermometer and monitor air temperatures with digital probes.

## Humidity
Maintain high humidity, generally 70-80% measured near the cool side at ground level. Nighttime rises and daytime dips are normal. Keep substrate evenly moist but not muddy, mist with a pressure sprayer as needed, and provide a humid hide with damp sphagnum moss. Humid hides can run around 90-100%, but the moss must be replaced regularly to prevent mold.

## Substrate
Use at least 6 inches of clean, chemical-free soil-based substrate. A practical mix is organic topsoil with play sand, optionally blended with a reptile soil product; an 80% topsoil and 20% sand mix can work in a pinch. Add leaf litter to improve humidity and foraging behavior. Spot-clean daily, remove contaminated substrate, and replace the full substrate every 4-6 months depending on cleanliness and enclosure conditions. Use paper towels temporarily for sick or wounded animals.

## Decor and Enrichment
Argentine tegus need sturdy, large-scale furnishings. Provide a hide that fits the whole animal, additional caves, hollow logs, tree stumps, thick branches, large plants, and visual barriers. Arrange decor so the lizard can patrol, dig, climb low obstacles, and retreat from view. Heavy decor must be stable and protected from undermining.

## Diet
Argentine tegus are omnivores. Feed hatchlings about five times weekly, juveniles four times weekly, subadults three times weekly, and adults about twice weekly. Young tegus should receive roughly 70-80% protein with the rest vegetables and fruit; adults do well around 60% protein, 30% vegetables, and 10% fruit. Use varied proteins such as roaches, crickets, black soldier fly larvae, worms, snails, eggs with shell, appropriately sized rodents, chicks, fish, shrimp, and high-quality canned dog food. Offer greens and vegetables such as collards, cactus pads, spring mix, arugula, kale, bok choy, carrot tops, dandelion, and hibiscus. Fruit should be occasional.

## Supplements
Use calcium and multivitamin supplements, or a balanced all-in-one reptile supplement. Choose low-D3 or no-D3 calcium when UVB is properly provided, and follow product directions rather than over-dusting every meal.

## Water
Provide fresh water daily in a heavy container large enough for soaking. Many adult tegus need a shallow tub rather than a small bowl. Clean the water container often because tegus may soak, dig, and defecate in it.

## Safety Notes
Tegus are strong animals with powerful jaws and claws. Use secure locks, guarded fixtures, and stable decor. Do not free-roam unsupervised, and never rely on cohabitation for companionship. Large reptiles also require access to an experienced reptile veterinarian.
