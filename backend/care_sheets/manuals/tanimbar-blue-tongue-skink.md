# Tanimbar Blue Tongue Skink
**Scientific name:** Tiliqua scincoides chimaera
**Difficulty:** Intermediate

## Overview
Tanimbar blue tongue skinks are 18-24 inch terrestrial lizards from Indonesia. They have heavy bodies, strong short limbs, triangular heads, glossy scales, and blue-tinted tongues. Their coloration is often silver, gray, or yellowish. Unlike the northern blue tongue skink, this form comes from a humid tropical climate.

Tanimbars are less common in the pet trade than many other blue tongue skinks. Wild-caught individuals may be defensive and difficult to settle, while captive-bred animals are often more tolerant. With proper care, blue tongue skinks commonly live 15-20 years and may live longer.

## Enclosure
Use a front-opening enclosure at least 48 x 24 x 24 inches for one adult, with larger housing preferred. Provide good ventilation while still supporting tropical humidity. Keep only one Tanimbar blue tongue skink per enclosure; cohabitation can result in severe fighting.

## Lighting and UVB
Tanimbar blue tongue skinks are diurnal and benefit from bright daytime light. UVB is strongly recommended for welfare even if dietary D3 is used. Provide a 22 inch T5 HO desert-style UVB lamp, such as a 10.0 or 12% bulb, over the warm side. Aim for a basking UVI around 3.0-4.0. Approximate basking distances are 9-14 inches when the lamp is above mesh and 15-24 inches when mounted without mesh obstruction. Measure with a UV meter when possible.

## Heating
Provide a halogen basking area with a surface temperature of 100-105°F and a cool side of 70-80°F. Use a broad stone or tile basking surface and measure it with an infrared thermometer. A digital probe thermometer can passively monitor the warm zone. Use dimmers or different bulb wattage to tune temperatures.

## Humidity
Maintain average humidity around 60-80%, measured near the cool side at ground level. Mist in the morning and evening as needed. If extra nighttime humidity is required, a cool-mist humidifier or fogger can be used for a limited period after lights out. Use distilled water in foggers and disinfect them regularly. Do not run foggers during the day, as warm stagnant mist can contribute to respiratory problems.

## Substrate
Use 4-6 inches of moisture-retentive soil-based substrate with leaf litter. Commercial tropical reptile soils, coconut fiber blends, cypress or similar mulch used appropriately, or a DIY mix of about 40% organic topsoil, 40% reptile soil, and 20% play sand can work. Add clean leaf litter and optional sphagnum moss. Spot-clean daily and replace the full substrate every 4-6 months as needed. Use paper towels temporarily for sick or injured skinks.

## Decor and Enrichment
Provide at least two hides, cork logs, cork flats, sturdy branches, rocks, and durable live or artificial plants. Arrange cover so the skink can move between warm, cool, humid, and drier areas without being fully exposed. All decor should be stable because blue tongue skinks are powerful diggers and pushers.

## Diet
Tanimbar blue tongue skinks are omnivores. Babies up to 3 months can eat daily, juveniles 3-8 months about three times weekly, and subadults or adults 1-2 times weekly. Skinks under 12 months should receive a high-protein diet, roughly 70-80% animal foods. Adults usually do well with about 50-60% animal foods and the remainder leafy greens and vegetables, with fruit as a treat.

Protein options include high-quality dog or cat food without fish flavors, eggs, chicken hearts, chicken gizzards, snails, dubia roaches, grasshoppers, locusts, hornworms, silkworms, earthworms, and occasional appropriately sized whole prey. Plant foods include collards, cactus pads, spring mix, arugula, kale, pea shoots, alfalfa, bok choy, carrot greens, dandelion greens and flowers, hibiscus, and small amounts of fruit such as berries, fig, apple, papaya, mango, or prickly pear.

## Supplements
Use calcium and a reptile multivitamin, or a balanced all-in-one supplement, as directed. Avoid excessive D3 when reliable UVB is provided unless advised by a reptile veterinarian.

## Water
Provide a large shallow bowl of fresh water at all times. Replace daily or when soiled.

## Safety Notes
Avoid cohabitation and use caution with defensive wild-caught animals. Maintain humidity without making the enclosure wet or stagnant. Keep heat lamps out of reach and check temperatures frequently to prevent burns or overheating.
