# Red-Eared Slider
**Scientific name:** Trachemys scripta elegans
**Difficulty:** Hard

## Overview
Red-eared sliders are aquatic turtles that commonly reach 10-12 inches, with females usually larger than males. They are dark green to yellowish with marbled markings and a red stripe behind each eye. Their native range is in the southern and south-central United States, but released pets have established invasive populations in many regions.

This species can live 20-30 years or longer. Although hardy, red-eared sliders are demanding pets because they need very large water volume, strong filtration, high-output lighting, a dry basking area, and long-term maintenance.

## Enclosure
Use at least 10 gallons of water per inch of shell length. Most adults need 100-120 gallons of water or more. Aquariums, stock tanks, indoor ponds, and outdoor ponds can work if they are secure, watertight, and escape-proof. Ponds or large tubs are often more practical than glass tanks for adults. Keep one slider per enclosure; group housing in small indoor systems often causes bullying and competition.

## Lighting and UVB
Provide 12 hours of bright daytime lighting. Use a T5 HO 5.0 or 6% UVB lamp in a reflective fixture over the basking area. In a 48 inch setup, a 34 inch lamp placed about 13-14 inches above the basking surface often produces an appropriate basking UVI around 3.0-4.0, but a UV meter is the best way to confirm.

Add a bright 6500K LED or fluorescent daylight fixture spanning about half to three quarters of the enclosure, especially if live plants are used. Visible light supports activity but does not replace UVB.

## Heating
Provide both basking heat and heated water. Target a basking surface of 95-104°F, basking air of 85-90°F, and water temperature of 74-76°F. Use splash-resistant halogen basking lamps over a dry platform large enough for the turtle to fully leave the water and dry off. Measure basking surfaces with an infrared thermometer and water with a submersible digital thermometer. Use an appropriately rated aquarium heater with a protective guard.

## Humidity
Room humidity is usually adequate because the enclosure is aquatic. Good airflow above the basking area is important so the turtle can dry fully. Avoid enclosing the basking platform in stagnant, damp air.

## Substrate
Substrate is optional in the water area. Avoid gravel or small pebbles because they can be swallowed. Bare-bottom tanks are easiest to clean; sand or large river stones can be used if maintenance remains manageable. A land area may be filled with play sand, with a flat stone under the heat lamps for basking.

## Decor and Enrichment
Use driftwood, large rocks, underwater hides, sturdy live plants, and safe floating objects to encourage swimming, climbing, and exploration. Any decor must be stable and too large to swallow. Compatible live fish may provide enrichment, but they may be eaten and should never introduce parasites or disease.

## Diet
Red-eared sliders are omnivores. Turtles under 1 year can receive about 50% protein and 50% vegetables, with protein and vegetables daily and pellets every other day. Adults over 1 year should shift toward roughly 25% protein and 75% vegetables, with protein 1-2 times weekly, vegetables daily, and pellets 2-3 times weekly.

A protein portion is what the turtle can eat in 5-10 minutes. A vegetable portion can be about shell-sized, and a pellet portion about head-sized. Protein options include crickets, earthworms, roaches, silkworms, snails, frozen bloodworms, freeze-dried shrimp or krill, and appropriate feeder fish such as guppies or mollies. Plant foods include collards, dandelion greens and flowers, endive, green or red leaf lettuce, romaine, kale, grated squash, carrot, green beans, and grated sweet potato. Fruit should be rare.

## Supplements
Quality turtle pellets provide many vitamins and minerals, but offer calcium with cuttlebone. Remove the hard backing before placing it in the enclosure. Replace cuttlebone as it is consumed or fouled, often every 1-2 months.

## Water
Clean water is a major husbandry requirement. Use a canister filter rated for at least 2-3 times the actual water volume. Replace about 30% of the water every 1-2 weeks, and test water quality when problems arise. Use a siphon or pump for water changes and disinfect any sink or tub used for dirty turtle water.

## Safety Notes
Never release a pet slider outdoors. Use a GFCI outlet for electrical safety near water. Protect heaters from contact burns, secure all lights above splash range, and wash hands and equipment after working in the enclosure.
