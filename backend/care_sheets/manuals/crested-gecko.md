# Crested Gecko
**Scientific name:** Correlophus ciliatus
**Difficulty:** Easy

## Overview
Crested geckos are small arboreal lizards from New Caledonia. Adults are usually 6-8 inches long, with broad toe pads, a soft crest running from the eyes down the body, and a wide range of captive-bred colors and patterns. They live in humid forest habitats and spend most of their time climbing among branches and foliage. With appropriate care, they often live 15-20 years or longer.

## Enclosure
A single adult should have a front-opening vertical enclosure at least 18 inches long, 18 inches wide, and 24 inches tall. Bigger is better if the space is well planted and furnished. Very small juveniles under about 12 grams may do better in a smaller temporary grow-out enclosure until they can reliably find food and navigate a larger habitat. Cohabitation is not recommended for beginners; males should never be housed together.

## Lighting and UVB
Crested geckos are crepuscular and need a gentle day/night cycle. UVB is optional for survival when they eat a balanced commercial diet, but low-level UVB is beneficial. Use a 5-6% T8 UVB bulb in a reflective fixture and keep the closest basking area at least 6 inches below the lamp. Aim for UVI around 1.0-2.0 near the upper basking zone. Provide shaded retreats so the gecko can avoid light when resting.

## Heating
Crested geckos prefer moderate temperatures. Target a basking area of 82-85°F, a general cool zone of 70-75°F, and nighttime temperatures of 65-72°F. A low-wattage white incandescent bulb is often enough if the room is cool. Use a dimmer or lower wattage if the top of the enclosure gets too warm. Provide branches, vines, and foliage at multiple heights so the gecko can choose warmer or cooler areas.

## Humidity
Maintain average humidity around 60-80%, with natural rises at night and drops during the day. Mist the enclosure in the morning and evening with a pressure sprayer to raise humidity and provide droplets for drinking. Avoid keeping the enclosure constantly saturated; airflow and a daytime drying period help prevent mold and skin problems.

## Substrate
Use 2-4 inches of moisture-retentive tropical substrate to stabilize humidity and cushion falls. A mix of organic topsoil and coconut fiber works, as do reptile soil or tropical bioactive-style mixes. Add leaf litter over the top. Spot-clean waste daily and replace substrate every 1-3 months unless a well-maintained bioactive system is used.

## Decor and Enrichment
The enclosure should be densely furnished from bottom to top. Use branches, vines, cork, bamboo, arboreal hides, and live or artificial foliage. Provide sleeping cover, feeding ledges, and multiple climbing routes. A sparse enclosure leaves crested geckos exposed and can reduce normal behavior.

## Diet
Crested geckos are omnivores that eat fruit-based foods and insects. Use a high-quality complete crested gecko diet mixed with water as the staple. Feed hatchlings and juveniles prepared diet daily and insects 1-2 times weekly. Feed adults prepared diet every 2-3 days and insects about once weekly. Good feeder insects include crickets, dubia roaches, red runner roaches, darkling beetles, snails, and grasshoppers. Rotate prepared diet flavors or brands when possible to encourage variety.

## Supplements
Complete crested gecko diets are already fortified. Dust feeder insects lightly with calcium without D3 unless veterinary guidance or UVB conditions suggest otherwise. Follow supplement labels and avoid adding extra vitamins directly to the prepared diet unless instructed.

## Water
Mist-created droplets are an important drinking source, but a small water cup can also be provided and refreshed regularly. Keep feeding cups and ledges clean because sweet diets spoil quickly.

## Safety Notes
Crested geckos can drop their tails, and dropped tails do not regrow. Handle gently, close to a surface, and avoid grabbing the tail. Temperatures above the safe range can become dangerous quickly, so monitor warm rooms carefully.
