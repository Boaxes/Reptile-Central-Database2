# Ocellated Skink
**Scientific name:** Chalcides ocellatus
**Difficulty:** Easy

## Overview
Ocellated skinks are small, smooth-scaled lizards that usually reach 7-9 inches long. They have cylindrical bodies, tapered tails, small limbs, and a tan to bronze base color marked with pale and dark eye-like spots. Their range includes Mediterranean coastal regions, parts of western Africa, areas near the Red Sea, and coastal portions of the Arabian Peninsula.

They are adaptable, active, and enjoyable to observe, but they are not ideal handling pets. In captivity they do best in dry, bright, sandy setups with a strong basking area and enough loose substrate for burrowing.

## Enclosure
One adult can be kept in an enclosure at least 30 x 12 x 12 inches, though larger is better. A front-opening enclosure with strong ventilation works well. Groups are possible but not necessary; if keeping up to three adults, use at least a 36 x 18 x 16 inch enclosure and monitor closely for stress, competition, or injuries.

## Lighting and UVB
Ocellated skinks are diurnal and should have bright visible light plus UVB. Use a 12-hour photoperiod, or a seasonal range from about 10 hours in winter to 14 hours in summer. Provide a T5 HO 5.0 or 6% UVB lamp over the warm side. Aim for a basking UVI of 2.0-3.0. Depending on mesh and fixture placement, the basking platform may need to sit roughly 9-14 inches below the UVB lamp.

Bright 6000-7000K daylight from a separate LED or fluorescent fixture supports activity and appetite. This visible-light lamp does not replace UVB.

## Heating
Create a hot basking zone with a halogen flood lamp. Target a basking surface of 110-120°F, basking air of 90-100°F, and a cool side of 75-85°F. Use a flat piece of slate, sandstone, or flagstone large enough for the whole skink. Measure the surface with an infrared thermometer and use a dimmer or different wattage bulb to adjust.

## Humidity
Daytime humidity should stay low, around 15-35%, with a nighttime spike up to 90-100% after misting. Measure humidity in the middle of the enclosure. Evening misting should dampen the surface without leaving the enclosure wet all day.

## Substrate
Use about 4 inches of fine sand or sandy soil so the skinks can burrow. Suitable options include reptile-safe desert sand, sandy arid blends, or clean play sand. Remove waste daily, scoop contaminated areas, and replace the full substrate every 3-6 months depending on cleanliness.

## Decor and Enrichment
Offer cork hollows, cholla wood, sturdy branches, ledges, rockwork, drought-tolerant live plants, artificial plants, and a textured background if available. Arrange decor so the skink can move between cover, basking, and cooler areas without feeling exposed.

## Diet
Ocellated skinks are primarily insectivores. Babies can eat daily, juveniles and adults 2-3 times weekly, and pregnant or recently postpartum females daily. Offer as many appropriately sized feeders as the skink will take in about five minutes. Finely chopped greens or vegetables can be offered up to twice weekly.

Useful feeders include small crickets, bean beetles, dubia or discoid roaches, red runner roaches, fruit flies, hornworms, silkworms, mealworms, darkling beetles, isopods, rice flour beetles, and rice worms. Occasional commercial insect-based diets may add variety. Plant options include arugula, basil, carrot greens, grated carrot, chard, cilantro, collard greens, dandelion greens and flowers, hibiscus, endive, kale, mustard greens, and parsley.

## Supplements
Dust feeders with calcium and use a reptile multivitamin or balanced all-in-one supplement according to label directions. Gut-load insects before feeding for better nutrition.

## Water
Provide a shallow bowl of clean water at all times. Replace daily and after substrate is kicked into it.

## Safety Notes
Avoid excessive handling, as these skinks are small and fast. Do not allow the enclosure to remain damp during the day. Monitor group-housed animals closely and separate any skink that is being chased, bitten, blocked from food, or losing weight.
